FDA adverse-event reports

For drugs that have a US product label on openFDA. Follows the drug filter above; phase, status and sponsor filters do not apply here.

These are counts of reports, not rates — they cannot be compared as risk

A bigger number does not mean a drug is less safe. Reporting by health professionals and patients is voluntary, and the reports are not checked: they cannot be used to estimate the incidence of these events [2]. Common products may have more reports simply because more people use them, and a report can name several drugs and several reactions with no way to conclude which drug caused which reaction [2]. Counts here include reports that name the drug as suspect or as a concomitant medicine, and only reports openFDA could match to the product (harmonised fields return only a subset of records [5]). The data may lag by 3 months or more [2], so the latest year is incomplete.

Where the numbers come from

Every number on this page is computed in your browser from the responses below, as fetched by this app's server. Nothing is typed in by hand.

ClinicalTrials.gov API v2

One search per drug on the intervention field, names and code names joined with OR. Re-fetched every 12 hours.

openFDA

Drug adverse events (FAERS) and drug labels. Re-fetched every 24 hours.

How each number is calculated, and the limits
  • Which trials: a study is included when ClinicalTrials.gov's intervention search matches the drug's name, brand names or code name (the queries are listed above). That includes trials where the drug is only the comparator, and some observational studies. The search is ClinicalTrials.gov's own, so it can include a record that only mentions the drug in passing.
  • Counting once: headline numbers, the sponsor table, the map and the trial list count each NCT number once. Charts broken down by drug count a trial under every drug it lists, so their rows add up to more than the headline total.
  • Active = recruiting, not yet recruiting, active and not recruiting, or enrolling by invitation. Stopped = terminated, withdrawn or suspended. Unknown is ClinicalTrials.gov's own status for records not updated after their completion date passed.
  • Phase 3 includes trials registered as phase 2/3.
  • Participants add up the enrollment field of interventional trials only (observational records are often database studies of whole populations): actual numbers for finished trials, targets ("estimated") for trials still enrolling. "Estimated" is the planned target: mostly trials still enrolling, but also records (often status Unknown) that never reported a final number. Withdrawn trials usually show zero.
  • Cities are distinct city-and-country names among locations that have coordinates (map dots are the same cities); site listings count every location entry. Records without coordinates are left off the map but still count toward their country.
  • FDA report counts come from openFDA's count endpoints, with the drug matched on its harmonised generic name, and are grouped by the year FDA received the report. The share of serious reports and a reaction's share of a drug's reports are shares of reports, not rates in patients.
