Heating a home: gas furnace, heat pump or electric baseboard

November 2026 · United States figures

A heat pump moves heat instead of making it, so it usually needs the least energy and also cools in summer. A gas furnace burns fuel; new efficient models turn almost all of it into heat but need a gas line and a flue. Electric baseboard is cheapest to install but uses the most electricity for the same warmth. Which is cheapest to run depends on your local gas and power prices.

Which fits

Gas furnace

You already have ducts and a gas line, gas is cheap where you live, and you only need heat.

Heat pump

You want heating and cooling from one system and lower energy use. Ductless mini-splits suit homes without ducts.[2]

Electric baseboard

A small space, an addition or a mild climate where low install cost matters more than the power bill.

Install and running costs above are relative, not dollar amounts: they vary too much by region and home to quote one figure. Get local quotes and compare against your gas and electricity rates.
