# Competitor analysis

Use `deep-research` to gather the evidence and `sourced-report` to write it. This skill says
what a complete analysis contains.

## Research threads (one sub-agent each)
1. **The company itself**: latest annual and quarterly report (10-K, 10-Q, annual report),
   investor presentation. Revenue, margin, segments, geography, headcount, guidance.
2. **Products and pricing**: the company's own product pages and price lists; launches in the last two years.
3. **Each main competitor** (two to four): the same financial and product facts, from their own filings.
4. **Market**: size, growth, share estimates, regulation and policy; name the analyst or agency behind each estimate.
5. **Customers and channel**: reviews, partner or reseller commentary, complaints, switching reasons.
   Treat forums and reviews as opinion and label them so.

## Sections of the page
1. **Summary**: the position in five sentences, and the three things that matter most.
2. **Company snapshot**: key figures table (revenue, growth, gross margin, operating income,
   cash, employees), all for the same fiscal period, each marked.
3. **How it makes money**: segments, products, pricing model.
4. **Competitors**: one comparison table with the same rows for every company and the fiscal
   period in the column header. Where a company does not disclose a number, write "not disclosed".
5. **Charts** (three to five, only where a picture shows more than the table): revenue over
   time, margin comparison, segment or geographic mix, share. Each with its data table.
6. **Strengths and weaknesses** of the company against each competitor, each point tied to evidence above.
7. **Risks and what to watch**: policy, pricing pressure, technology shifts, concentration.
8. **What could not be confirmed**.
9. **Sources** and **Checked numbers**.

## Standards
- Fiscal years differ between companies: state each company's year end; never compare a
  calendar year with a fiscal year silently.
- Convert currencies only with a stated rate and date, and mark the result as derived.
- Market-share figures are estimates: name who estimated them and when.
- Do not characterise a company's strategy or problems without a source; a quoted statement
  from management or a named analyst is the evidence.
